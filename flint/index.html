How Drying Works on a Flint Water Damage Job

Touch is a poor test for whether a wall or floor has dried. The face of drywall often seems normal when the cavity side is damp, and carpet fibers dry long before the padding underneath. That is why professional drying runs on instruments. In Flint we use pin meters that measure moisture inside wood and drywall, non-invasive meters that scan behind surfaces, and thermal cameras that show cool, damp patches.

Different materials let go of water at different speeds. Drywall and carpet give up moisture fast, but studs, subflooring, hardwood and concrete are slow. The layout of dehumidifiers and air movers follows those rates and is changed as each day's numbers come in. Most jobs finish in 3 to 5 days, and if yours will take longer you will hear it from us at the start.

Stopping early is the costly mistake. Damp materials sealed behind new finishes may mold, buckle or delaminate months afterward. So for every Flint job we dry to a measured target and keep the readings on file.

Preventing Water Damage in Flint Homes

A large share of the jobs we attend in Flint began as a minor fault that could have been caught. Looking over a short list twice a year is enough to find most of those faults.

Start with supply lines. Washer, dishwasher, toilet and refrigerator lines made of plain rubber age badly, and swapping them for braided steel is a cheap fix. Look under every sink for stains, swollen cabinet floors or a musty smell. Check the water heater for rust at the base and drain a little from the tank once a year to clear sediment. Clean gutters and downspouts that discharge well away from the house keep rain out of the foundation.

Know where your main shutoff valve is and make sure everyone in the house can turn it. Leak sensors placed by the water heater, washer and under sinks cost little and will alert you early. If you find something in your Flint home that worries you, such as a stain that keeps growing, have it looked at before it becomes a cleanup.

Water Damage We See Most in Flint

Some water problems need a phone call this minute, and others can safely wait until morning. We have ordered these five common Flint calls by how fast each one needs attention.

Bursts: Nothing is more time-critical than a broken water line, which keeps pouring until the supply is cut. Cold weather, depending on the season, is one trigger, with the leak appearing as the frozen section thaws. Shut the house supply, run a tap to drain the lines and phone us immediately, whatever the hour. Ceilings that bulge with trapped water should be left alone and the room below kept empty.

Runoff: Storm water coming under a door or through a garage is next in line. There is no valve to close here, so lift what you can off the floor and avoid wet electrical outlets. Having passed over pavement and soil on the way in, it carries dirt and germs. The Warren Restoration team extracts it once conditions are safe, then removes soaked materials that cannot be cleaned.

Kitchens: A dishwasher that leaks from its door or a refrigerator line that drips behind the unit calls for same-day attention. Turning off the supply valve for that appliance normally ends the leak itself. The harm continues, though, since water has already run under cabinets and flooring where air does not move. In Flint kitchens we often dry cabinets in place by sending air through small holes in the toe kick.

Crawlspaces: Dampness in the space below the floor can wait a few days, though not a few months. When we look underneath, we tend to find water lying on the liner, insulation that is soaked or sweating ducts. Left alone, the damp air rises into the rooms above and the wood framing begins to decay. We extract the water, dry the wood to a safe reading and tell you what we think the source is.

Odors: Last on the list is a stale, earthy odor in a room that once had a leak, which is worth a scheduled visit. Since growth can start 24 to 48 hours after materials get wet, an old leak that was never fully dried is a likely culprit. Avoid wiping or bleaching a big area on your own, as that sends spores into other rooms. We check for hidden dampness, set up containment and clear out the affected material for homeowners in Flint.
