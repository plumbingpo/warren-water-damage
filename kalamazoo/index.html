What Usually Goes Wrong in Kalamazoo Homes

Water usually escapes at the same few vulnerable spots in a home. The list below covers what we are asked to clean up most for Kalamazoo owners.

Water heaters: Sediment collects in the bottom of a tank and slowly eats through the steel. Look for moisture at the base, rust streaks on the jacket or a small pool that keeps coming back. A split tank does not stop at one tankful, because the inlet keeps feeding it until the valve is shut. When the unit lives in an attic or interior closet, the water reaches carpets and ceilings far from it.

Roofs: One strong storm is enough to peel back shingles and push water beneath the roof covering. Leaks also start around chimneys, vent pipes and skylights where the sealant has dried out. Since water follows the framing downhill, the ceiling stain rarely sits directly below the actual opening. For roof leaks in Kalamazoo, we tarp where needed, pull out soaked insulation and dry the structure so the ceiling can be repaired.

Freezing: Cold nights, depending on the season, can break plumbing that showed no sign of trouble earlier. Outdoor spigots, sprinkler feeds and pipes in crawl spaces or along outside walls are hit first. Vacant homes and rooms with the heat turned down are especially exposed. No flow or a bare trickle at the tap on a frigid day means a frozen line, so close the main and get help.

Backups: Gurgling sounds and more than one slow fixture at once suggest the main drain is partly blocked. Once it closes off fully, anything you flush or drain returns at the lowest opening in the house. Since the waste is hazardous, we extract it and then sanitize concrete, tile and other hard surfaces. Stay out of the affected rooms and avoid running water until the line is cleared.

Seepage: Dampness at the base of a basement or foundation wall often means water is collecting outside. Short downspouts, soil that slopes toward the house and blocked footing drains are frequent causes. Early clues include powdery white residue on the concrete, flaking wall paint and air that always smells musty.
