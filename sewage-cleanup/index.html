A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. Across Warren, Warren Restoration answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

Sewage damage is classified as Category 3 (black water), the most contaminated and dangerous category of water damage. This classification requires the most stringent cleanup protocols, including full personal protective equipment, containment barriers, removal of all porous materials that contacted sewage and thorough disinfection of every remaining surface. Our technicians follow IICRC S500 standards for Category 3 water damage so your property is restored to safe occupancy standards.

Here is what the work covers: standing sewage is pumped out, contaminated water is extracted from the structure, porous materials the sewage touched (drywall, insulation, carpet and padding) are torn out and disposed of properly, hard surfaces are disinfected thoroughly, antimicrobials are applied, and the structure is dried so mold does not follow. Lingering sewage odor is eliminated with thermal fogging and industrial hydroxyl generators, which we run in the affected area.

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Route sump pump and gutter water away from the sewer line, which can back up when storm flow overloads it
  • Keep cooking oil, grease and food scraps out of the drain, where they build into clogs
  • Fit a strainer over each sink and shower drain so hair and debris stay out of the pipes
  • Schedule a camera inspection of the sewer line every 2 to 3 years so trouble is found early
  • Protect against municipal sewer backups by having a backwater valve fitted where your line connects
  • Have roots removed where trees grow by the sewer, before they get inside the pipe and plug it

Why Sewage Cleanup Is Not a DIY Job

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Professional sewage cleanup also requires proper disposal of contaminated materials as biohazard waste. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.

How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Sluggish drainage at all fixtures, a sign the main line is blocking up
  • Dark, dirty water or sewage rising out of a tub, toilet or floor drain
  • A toilet overflow containing waste that has spread onto flooring or carpet
  • More than one drain in the house overflowing or backing up together
  • A cleanout that weeps sewage, or wet foul patches in the lawn along the sewer path
  • Toilets or drains that gurgle whenever you run water somewhere else
  • Unexplained illness or gastrointestinal symptoms among household members
