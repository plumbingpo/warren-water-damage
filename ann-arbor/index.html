Drying Out an Ann Arbor Property: What to Expect

A surface that feels dry is not proof that a room is dry. Carpet can be dry on top while the pad is soaked, and painted drywall can look fine while the back of it is wet. That is why professional drying runs on instruments. In Ann Arbor we use pin meters that measure moisture inside wood and drywall, non-invasive meters that scan behind surfaces, and thermal cameras that show cool, damp patches.

Different materials let go of water at different speeds. Carpet and drywall respond quickly, while framing lumber, subfloor, hardwood and concrete hold on much longer. The layout of dehumidifiers and air movers follows those rates and is changed as each day's numbers come in. A span of 3 to 5 days covers most jobs, and we will tell you early if yours looks like an exception.

Stopping early is the costly mistake. Materials closed up while still damp can grow mold, swell, or come loose months later, long after the job seemed finished. Every Ann Arbor job is therefore dried to a recorded target, and the log stays on file.

What Usually Goes Wrong in Ann Arbor Homes

Most water damage calls we take in Ann Arbor trace back to a short list of causes. Knowing them makes it easier to catch trouble while it is still a small repair.

Pipes: When water freezes inside a line it can crack the metal or plastic, then pour out as soon as things warm up. Depending on the season, the plumbing most at risk runs through outside walls, attics and garages without heat. In older homes, aging joints and tired connectors sometimes fail with no cold snap involved. Your first move is closing the main water valve, since a broken pipe floods room after room fast.

Appliances: The thin hoses feeding a washer, dishwasher or ice maker do not last forever. Tanks rust from the inside, so an old water heater may drip for weeks or give out suddenly. We see many of these in Ann Arbor homes where the unit sits upstairs or over a finished room.

Overflows: It takes only a few minutes for a blocked toilet or a forgotten faucet to flood a bathroom. From there it slips beneath the trim and can drip into whatever sits under that floor. If the overflow came from the toilet bowl or a drain, treat it as contaminated and keep children and pets away.

Roof: Rain gets in through worn flashing, damaged shingles or gutters that have stopped draining. By the time a brown ring appears overhead, the attic insulation has often been wet for a while. We dry the framing, insulation and drywall, and a roofer needs to fix the opening so it does not return.

Basement: In homes that have a basement, groundwater pushes through foundation cracks and floor joints when the soil is saturated. If the sump pump sticks or the electricity goes out mid-storm, the pit fills and spills across the slab. Finished basements suffer most, since carpet, drywall and framing all soak up water from the bottom.

Mold: Mold can start growing within 24 to 48 hours on materials that stay wet. Many mold jobs we handle in Ann Arbor began with an old leak that was wiped up but never dried inside the wall. A musty smell, peeling paint or dark spotting near a previous repair is worth having checked.

Insurance and Water Damage: A Guide for Ann Arbor Owners

Adjusters work from evidence, so the strongest thing an Ann Arbor homeowner can bring to a water damage claim is a good record. Start building it before anything is cleaned up or thrown away.

Photograph the source of the water, such as the split hose or the failed fitting, and keep the broken part if you can. Shoot every room from the doorway, then move in for close-ups of ruined belongings and the model numbers on electrical goods. Hold damaged flooring and possessions until your adjuster has inspected them or said that pictures are enough. Save every receipt connected to the damage, including emergency supplies and temporary lodging.

Many policies treat an abrupt failure differently from long-term seepage or flood water coming in from outdoors. Where your loss falls is for the insurer or your agent to say, and it is not something we can promise. Our crew supplies the technical record, meaning moisture maps, drying logs and a plain description of what we found.
