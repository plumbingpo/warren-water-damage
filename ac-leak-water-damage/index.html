AC Leak Water Damage Help in Warren

Cooling a house also dries the air, which means the unit is constantly making water it must get rid of. In a healthy system the coil sheds it into a pan, and a narrow drain line carries it away. Once dust and algae block the drain, water backs up until it runs over the edge of the pan. With an air handler in the attic or a hallway closet, the overflow goes straight into the ceiling, wall or floor around it.

A slow drip causes more harm than most homeowners would guess. The water wicks into drywall, soaks the platform under the unit and creeps under flooring, often with mold already started by the time a stain shows. Our team measures how far it traveled, dries the building materials and fixes the damage. Clearing the drain or repairing the unit remains a job for an HVAC technician, and our schedule can fit around theirs.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut the System DownSwitch the system off at the thermostat so it stops making condensate. Flip the air handler's breaker too when water is close to its wiring.
  2. Avoid Wet Wiring and LightsCeiling lights, outlets and the unit itself are off limits while wet, and stay that way until their circuit has been turned off.
  3. Empty the Drain PanIf you can reach the pan safely, soak up or wet-vac the standing water so it stops spilling over. Skip this step if it means crossing a soaked attic.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsBlot wet carpet, wipe hard floors, set a bucket under any ceiling drip, and move boxes, clothes and furniture away from the closet or the stained area.
  5. Call Both TradesAn HVAC technician should clear the drain line, and our team should find out where the water went. Keep the AC off until the repair is made.
Insurance

What About Insurance?

With AC leaks, insurers mostly look at how long the water was running. Water damage is paid for under many homeowners policies when the overflow was sudden and you responded as soon as you discovered it. Denial is possible when the adjuster concludes the leak ran for weeks or months, since that reads as a gradual or maintenance problem. Either way, the cost of fixing the AC unit is typically left out. Check your policy, and we can supply readings and photos for the claim.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • Loose or separated fittings on the condensate pipe that drip where nobody can see.
  • A condensate pump that burned out or lost power and stopped moving water.
  • A rusted, cracked or out of level drain pan that lets water escape.
  • A condensate drain line clogged with algae, dust and slime from the coil.
  • Missing insulation on cold lines and ductwork, so condensation forms and falls.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Nearby Flooring

Water runs out under the closet door and gets beneath carpet pad, laminate or hardwood. Hardwood cups when it is not dried, while laminate swells for good.

Ceilings Under the Unit

When the air handler is overhead, the ceiling under it takes the water. Staining is the early sign, sagging follows, and drywall left wet for days can come down into the room below.

Mold Near the Return

Within 24 to 48 hours mold can start on wet wood and drywall next to the unit, and the return may draw spores into the ducts and around the home.

Closet Walls and Platform

Closet units often sit on a wood platform over the return air box. Water rots that platform and wicks up the surrounding drywall, where it stays hidden behind the unit.

How We Fix It

How We Handle AC Leak Water Damage

1

Find Every Wet Area

Stains show only part of it, so we scan the area by the air handler, the walls or ceiling beside it and the nearby flooring with a thermal camera and moisture meters.

2

Remove Water and Wet Drywall

We extract the water sitting in the carpet, the platform and the pan. Saturated or moldy insulation and drywall are removed, which opens the cavity to dry air.

3

Dry the Structure

We monitor readings throughout the several days, usually 3 to 5, that the fans and dehumidifiers need. We place equipment so the HVAC technician can still reach the unit.

4

Clean and Restore

Since condensate left standing for days is seldom clean, we apply an antimicrobial to the surfaces it touched. Then we replace insulation, drywall, baseboard and flooring and repaint so the area matches.
