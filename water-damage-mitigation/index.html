Put simply, mitigation is the work of holding damage to its current size. Repair is a different job. It is everything done before the repair to protect what is still sound. In Warren, our team handles mitigation by controlling the source, removing the water, containing the wet area and stabilizing the structure. Good mitigation makes the later repair smaller, and now and then makes it unnecessary.

People often use mitigation and restoration to mean the same thing. Each is its own phase of the job. The emergency stage is mitigation, where we stop the loss, keep it from spreading and stabilize the house. Once the structure tests dry, restoration takes over and covers repairing or rebuilding finishes, drywall and floors that were damaged. Because an insurer lists each one separately as well, knowing where you stand in the process is helpful.

On a standard mitigation call we work through four items. Source control, so no new water enters. Extraction, so standing water is gone. Third is containment, where controlled airflow and plastic barriers keep contaminants and moisture away from clean rooms. Stabilization, which means removing materials that cannot be saved, treating surfaces with antimicrobial, and setting air movers and dehumidifiers. Furniture and personal items are protected too, either relocated or raised on blocks.

Warning Signs for Water Damage Mitigation

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Along the floor, water is travelling out of one room and into another.
  • A leak has been stopped but floors, walls or cabinets are still wet.
  • Following a water loss, indoor air stays damp and condensation forms on the glass.
  • The space under your cabinets in the bathroom or kitchen took on water you cannot get to.
  • The insurance company instructed you to take reasonable action against further damage.
  • An opening in the roof or at a window lets rainwater enter the building.
  • The carpet pad is soaked through even where the surface seems only damp.

Why Water Damage Mitigation Is Not a DIY Job

Doing mitigation well means making a string of quick judgment calls. What drywall stays and dries where it is, and what must be removed? Is the water Category 1, 2 or 3, and has it changed since the leak began? Is removing the cabinet toe kick necessary? We answer those questions with training and moisture readings rather than by the look or feel of a surface.

Good mitigation also protects your claim. Under most policies you must act reasonably to keep the damage from growing, with proof that you did. Arrival photos, daily moisture readings and an inventory of removed materials all go into our records. Together those records show the water's effect and our response to it. It also gives the repair contractor a clear starting point, with no wet surprises behind new drywall.

Water Damage Mitigation Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Seal gaps around windows, doors and roof penetrations before the next heavy rain arrives.
  • An automatic valve on the main line can cut the water as soon as it detects a leak.
  • Ground next to the foundation should fall away from it, which keeps water from collecting there.
  • Braided steel supply lines should take the place of rubber ones on the washing machine about every five years.
  • Downspouts should empty well clear of the foundation, and gutters need to stay free of debris.
  • Find the main water valve and make sure it has not seized.

What to Expect From Our Water Damage Mitigation

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle water damage mitigation from start to finish:

1

Stop the Water Source

Depending on the cause, our crew closes the supply, caps a failed line, or covers the entry point with tarps and boards. When the permanent repair calls for a roofer or plumber, we help schedule it.

2

Extract the Water

Standing water is removed with pumps and extractors, and carpet and pad are checked. Getting bulk water out quickly does more to limit damage than any amount of drying equipment alone.

3

Set Up Containment

Wet rooms are closed off from dry ones with barriers of plastic sheeting and controlled airflow. This keeps humidity, dust and any contaminants from travelling through the house while work is under way.

4

Stabilize the Structure

Materials past saving come out, an antimicrobial is applied to surfaces, and air movers plus dehumidifiers go in. Each visit includes logged moisture readings, and the structure is usually ready for repair work within 3 to 5 days.
